What am I looking at?

Green is brush, and the bare lines worn through it are trails (the key under the map shows the shades). The dots are animals, bigger and redder for a larger body. Each one walks between the ringed destinations and, when it gets thirsty, to the pond.

An animal takes the cheapest route for its size. Thick brush slows small animals far more than big ones, and animals knock brush down as they pass (only big ones can break through the thickest). Brush grows back.

So a route that has been used is cheaper for the next animal. Trails appear within a few hundred ticks, join into a network that leads to water, and grow over when nobody uses them.

The charts: brush regrows from its patchy start, but the trails hold mean thickness below the 1.0 it would reach with no animals. The cleared share is the land that is trail. The CV is high when thin trails cut through thick brush and near 0 when the brush is uniform.
